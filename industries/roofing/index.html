Roofing Companies

Roofing Operations,
Actually Organized.

Roofing companies run on handoffs — estimate to production, office to field, one crew to the next. When those handoffs aren't systemized, nothing catastrophic happens all at once. Things just quietly slip: a follow-up that doesn't happen, a job status nobody can find, a process three different people run three different ways.

The Friction

Where Roofing Operations
Commonly Break Down.

Every roofing company is a little different, but the breakdowns tend to cluster in the same few places.

Lead-to-Estimate

Leads come in fast and from everywhere — referrals, ads, insurance calls. Without a consistent intake and follow-up process, estimates go out inconsistently and follow-up depends on who remembers to do it.

Sales-to-Production Handoff

The information that closes a sale doesn't always make it to the crew that has to execute it. Job details, customer preferences, and site notes get lost somewhere between the estimate and the first day on the roof.

Office-to-Field Communication

Scheduling changes, material issues, and customer questions all have to travel between the field and the office in real time — and when that channel isn't systemized, everyone ends up playing phone tag.

How We Help

Systems Built Around
How Roofing Actually Runs.

We don't start by recommending software. We start by mapping how a job actually moves through your business today — from the first phone call to the final walkthrough — and figure out where it's actually breaking down versus where it just feels chaotic.

From there, the work usually involves cleaning up CRM adoption, documenting the handoff between sales and production, and building the project documentation and scheduling systems your team will actually use.

The Approach

People + Process + Technology
in Roofing Companies.

People

Roofing operations run on people first — sales reps, production managers, crew leads, the office staff holding it all together. Any system we build starts with understanding how they actually work day to day, not how an org chart says they should.

Process

Before we touch a tool, we map the actual path a job takes — lead to estimate to contract to production to completion — and find where that path breaks down or depends on one person remembering something.

Technology

Whatever CRM, scheduling tool, or project management system you're already using, we look at whether it's actually being used the way it was meant to be — and fix that before recommending something new.

Where to Start

Areas That May Be Worth Improving.

Every business is different — but these are the areas that come up most often.

Every lead gets followed up on, even during the busy weeks when it's easy for one to slip through
