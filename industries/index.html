Different Business.

No two businesses operate the same way day to day — but underneath the surface, the operational breakdowns tend to look strikingly similar: inconsistent processes, poor information flow, tools that exist but aren't really used, and a leadership team still approving things that shouldn't need them anymore. Roofing, real estate, and service businesses are where we've seen this most closely — but the pattern isn't industry-specific.

Industry Overview

Where We Most Often Come In.

Every industry has its own rhythm and its own vocabulary — but the friction underneath tends to fall into the same handful of patterns. Here's how that usually shows up in the businesses we work with most.

RF

Roofing Companies

Roofing Companies

Roofing operations move fast and involve a lot of handoffs — from the first estimate, to production, to the office, to whoever's fielding the customer's calls in between. When those handoffs aren't clearly systemized, things get dropped, and it's usually the customer who notices first.

We work inside that chain — cleaning up how leads and estimates get followed up on, how information moves between sales and production, and how the office stays looped in without everything running through one person.

Talk to Katie About Roofing Operations

Leads and estimates that don't get consistent follow-up
Handoffs between sales, production, and the office that lose information along the way
A CRM that's in place but not actually being used
Project documentation that lives in someone's head — or a truck
Scheduling and communication that depends on who you catch on the phone
SOPs that exist on paper but aren't followed day to day
Leadership fielding decisions that should run without them
RE

Real Estate Professionals

Real Estate Professionals

Agents and teams juggle a lot at once — leads at every stage, transactions on different timelines, and a client experience that's supposed to feel seamless even when the backend isn't. When the systems behind it are inconsistent, it shows up as dropped follow-up and a transaction process that depends on memory.

We restructure the database and CRM, build repeatable systems for onboarding and follow-up, and streamline the administrative side of transactions so less depends on manual coordination.

Talk to Katie About Real Estate Systems

Leads that go cold because follow-up isn't systemized
A client journey that's inconsistent from one transaction to the next
A CRM or database that's outdated or underused
Transaction paperwork and deadlines tracked manually
Onboarding that has to be re-explained to every new hire or assistant
Administrative work that eats time that should go to clients
SB

Service Businesses

Service Businesses

This category is intentionally broad — if your business runs on appointments, projects, or client work, the operational patterns tend to look similar no matter what the service actually is. Intake is inconsistent, scheduling depends on one person, and the client experience varies by who's handling it that day.

We organize intake and scheduling, document the handoffs between team members, and build the systems — and automation, where it actually helps — that make the client experience consistent no matter who's involved.

Talk to Katie About Your Business

Intake that looks different depending on who answers
Scheduling and communication that leans on one person to hold it together
Internal handoffs where information gets lost
Little to no documentation of how things are actually supposed to run
A client experience that depends on who's handling it that day
Manual work that could be simplified or automated, but hasn't been yet
