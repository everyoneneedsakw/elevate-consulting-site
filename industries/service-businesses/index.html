The Friction

Where Service Businesses
Commonly Break Down.

This looks different depending on the business, but the underlying patterns tend to repeat.

Inconsistent Intake

How a new inquiry gets handled depends entirely on who answers — the questions asked, the information collected, and the first impression a potential client gets all vary.

Scheduling That Leans on One Person

One person holds the calendar, the client communication, and the internal handoffs together — which works until they're out, overwhelmed, or gone.

Undocumented Handoffs

Work moves between team members with no clear process for what information needs to travel with it, so things get repeated, missed, or dropped.

How I Help

Systems Built Around
How Your Business Actually Runs.

Every service business is a little different, so I start by understanding how work actually moves through yours today — intake, scheduling, delivery, follow-up — before recommending anything.

From there, it's usually some combination of documenting the handoffs, organizing intake and scheduling, and building in automation where it actually reduces manual work instead of adding another tool to manage.
